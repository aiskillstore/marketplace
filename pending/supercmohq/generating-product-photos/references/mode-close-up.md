# Close-up / macro

**Use when:** the crop is tight enough that the subject is some single aspect of the product, such as
a material, a finish, a seam or a working part, rather than the whole product standing in a setting.

## What this mode is

A tight crop on one part of the product, showing texture, material or construction. It answers what a
buyer would ask holding the thing in a shop: what does it feel like, how is it made, what makes it
worth the price.

**In frame:** one feature and the few centimetres around it.
**Avoid:** the whole product; a setting; more than one property competing in the same frame.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish its material and finish and its most visually
interesting detail. Which single feature the crop is about decides both the light angle and the plane
of focus, so settle that before writing.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Framing:** extreme close-up on the one feature the shot is about. Give how much of the frame it
fills and where the crop cuts across the object.

**Background:** what sits behind the feature, its tone, and how far it falls out of focus.

**Lighting:** raking light — one key set at a shallow angle to the surface, close to parallel with
it. Give the direction it comes from, whether it is hard or soft and how large it reads against the
crop, and colour temperature in Kelvin. That is the whole instruction. The shallow angle is what
makes texture read; the reference supplies the texture, so do not describe what the light finds.

**Camera and lens:** macro lens, focal length and aperture, the plane of focus, and the exact point
that must be sharpest. Depth of field is very shallow at this magnification, so say what is allowed
to fall away.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself — no colour, no material, no parts. The fixed block and the
  reference photo carry all of it. Naming which feature the crop sits on is pointing, not describing.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- One property per frame. Texture and polish in the same shot means neither reads.
- A broad, diffused source lights the same surface flat; raking light is what makes it read.
- Concrete and named: "hard source at 15° to the surface from camera-left", not "side lighting".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible at this magnification. There is very little background in frame.
