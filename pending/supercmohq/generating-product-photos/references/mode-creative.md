# Creative concept

**Use when:** the frame carries an idea about the product rather than a record of it — an unusual
angle, theatrical or coloured light, a graphic shadow, a staged impossibility, or whatever else
announces the frame as made. Typically key art, paid social, out-of-home.

## What this mode is

A frame built to stop a scroll by breaking the pattern of how this product, and this whole category,
is normally shown. It may be impossible: the product at the wrong scale, somewhere it could never be,
caught mid-transformation into something else, or surrounded by things that have no business being
near it. The break is the point — it buys attention before it explains anything.

**In frame:** whatever the idea requires.
**Avoid:** a merely well-lit or nicely angled frame, which is not a break in the pattern; an idea that
would work equally well for any other product; more than one departure competing in the same frame.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish what the product is made of, how it works and
what it does for whoever owns it — the idea has to come from one of those, or the frame is decoration
that would work for any product.

Then settle two things before writing a word of the prompt, and put neither of them in it:

- **The idea** — one sentence saying what the frame is about. Every choice below serves it.
- **The category's usual view** — how this kind of product is normally shown. The angle you pick only
  reads as a departure against it.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Shot type and composition:** the view, camera height, and where the product sits in the frame. It
must still be recognisable from the angle you pick.

**Action:** the one thing physically happening that could not really happen — the object, what it is
doing, and against what. One only, stated as a physical fact. A word like "surreal" describes
nothing.

**Set and background:** the surface and backdrop the action happens against, named colour and
material.

**Props and styling:** the supporting elements, each smaller, plainer in pattern and less saturated
than the product, so nothing competes with it.

**Lighting:** here it is part of the idea rather than illumination. The colour of each source, where
the highlights fall and what shape they are, and which parts of the frame stay unlit.

**Colour palette:** two or three dominant tones for the set, the lighting and the props, and how
they contrast. The product's own colours are not yours to set.

**Camera and lens:** focal length, aperture, and what stays sharp.

**Atmosphere:** the feeling the frame carries.

Anything held in the air carries a cast shadow offset from the point directly below it, and no
contact shadow.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself — no colour, no material, no parts. The fixed block and the
  reference photo carry all of it.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- Concrete and named: "warm mid-grey seamless paper", not "a neutral background".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible from the angle you asked for.
