# Lifestyle

**Use when:** the product sits in a real place — in use, or lived with in a space the buyer reads as
their own — and the frame sells the outcome rather than the object (not a person as the subject, not
a built set).

## What this mode is

The product in the place it is actually used, in the middle of being used. It sells the outcome —
what owning the thing is like — rather than the object itself.

**In frame:** a real setting, props belonging to whoever lives there, and optionally hands or part of
a person.
**Avoid:** a person as the subject; a set that reads as built for the camera; a room richer or poorer
than the buyer's own.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish what the product is for, who would own it and
its dominant colours. The room, the props and the palette all follow from that owner.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Shot type and composition:** the camera height, where the product sits in the scene, and how much
of the frame it takes.

**Setting:** the specific room or spot the product is genuinely used or kept in, how completely
furnished it is, and the foreground and background layers that give the room depth. Match it to the
buyer — a room richer or poorer than theirs reads as borrowed.

**Props and styling:** the few surrounding objects, all things this buyer would already own, plus any
signs of recent use.

**Action:** what is happening — a hand and what it is doing, or no people at all.

**Focal point:** what makes the product the subject in a frame full of competing objects. Name the
mechanism: a pool of light falling only there, isolation, contrast, or one plane of focus.

**Lighting:** the time of day, the motivated source in the scene it comes from, its direction and
quality, and colour temperature in Kelvin. One temperature for the product and the room alike.

**Camera and lens:** focal length, aperture, and what is sharp against what falls away.

**Atmosphere:** the feeling the frame leaves.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself — no colour, no material, no parts. The fixed block and the
  reference photo carry all of it.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- Concrete and named: "warm mid-grey seamless paper", not "a neutral background".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible from the angle you asked for.
