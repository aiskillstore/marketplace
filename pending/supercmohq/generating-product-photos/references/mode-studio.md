# Studio

**Use when:** the product stands on its own against a clean, controlled background, with no
environment and nothing in frame beyond what holds it up.

## What this mode is

The product alone against a plain, continuous background. Accuracy over aesthetics: shape, colour,
proportion and design shown exactly as they are, so a buyer knows what they are getting. This is the
packshot — the image a listing, a catalogue or a category grid runs.

**In frame:** the product, and whatever holds it up.
**Avoid:** props, hands, an environment, or anything else that is not the product.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish its dominant colours and tonal range, its
material and finish, and the state it is in. The background tone is chosen against its colours and
the lighting against its material.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Shot type and composition:** the view — straight on, three-quarter, or low — the camera height, and
how much of the frame the product fills.

**Background:** a seamless sweep, its colour and tone, running unbroken behind and beneath the
product with no seam, corner or edge. Set its tone against the product's own — a pale product needs a
darker ground, a dark product a lighter one — and say whether it sits brighter, level or darker in
exposure.

**Lighting:** where the key sits relative to the lens and how high above the product, whether it is
hard or soft and how large it reads against the product, and colour temperature in Kelvin. Choose it
for the product's material: gloss wants a large diffused source, texture a small hard one. Add a
second source only where the shadow side or the edge against the background needs it.

**Shadow:** contact shadow, no shadow, or a cast shadow used as a shape — with its direction and how
soft the edge is.

**Camera and lens:** focal length, aperture, and what has to stay sharp. Deep depth of field so the
whole product holds, not just its nearest face.

**Colour:** neutral white balance and no colour cast picked up from the background, so the product's
own colours reproduce as they are. Do not name them — the reference carries them.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself — no colour, no material, no parts. The fixed block and the
  reference photo carry all of it.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- Concrete and named: "warm mid-grey seamless paper", not "a neutral background".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible from the angle you asked for.
