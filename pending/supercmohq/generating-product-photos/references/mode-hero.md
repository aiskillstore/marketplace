# Hero shot

**Use when:** one art-directed frame has to carry a campaign — whatever single placement the campaign
leans on, an ad or a banner or an email header — with the product commanding the frame.

## What this mode is

One art-directed frame built to carry a campaign. The product is the subject and commands the frame,
styled for desire rather than accuracy: lit, staged and graded to make someone want it. It runs as
the lead image on an ad, a banner, an email header or a landing page.

**In frame:** the product, a controlled setting, at most a prop or two.
**Avoid:** a setting that tells its own story; a person as the subject; props that pull attention off
the product; anything that reads as a scene the product happens to be standing in.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish its material and finish and its most visually
interesting detail — at this size the product's own surfaces do most of the describing, so the light
is chosen for them.

Settle what the campaign is claiming before writing, and let it drive the angle, the light and the
atmosphere. The claim itself does not go in the prompt.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Shot type and composition:** the camera height, rule-of-thirds placement, and how much of the frame
the product fills. Where the brief says text will be added over the frame, say which side stays clear;
where it never mentions text, say nothing about it.

**Set and background:** the surface or backdrop, named colour and material.

**Lighting:** where the key sits and how high, graded so the frame is brighter at one edge and falls
off toward the other, and colour temperature in Kelvin. Say which part of the frame holds the
brightest value and which stays dark — as regions of the frame, not as marks on the product.

**Props and styling:** how few, how small, and what pairs them with the product — scale, colour or
texture.

**Depth:** a foreground or background element nearer or further from the lens than the product.
Without one a wide frame reads as empty.

**Camera and lens:** focal length, aperture, and what stays sharp.

**Atmosphere:** the feeling the frame carries.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself — no colour, no material, no parts. The fixed block and the
  reference photo carry all of it.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- Concrete and named: "warm mid-grey seamless paper", not "a neutral background".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible from the angle you asked for.
