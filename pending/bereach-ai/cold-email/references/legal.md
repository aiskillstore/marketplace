# Legal basics

Not legal advice. The rules below are what the regulators publish. When in doubt, ask a lawyer.

## By country of the recipient

| Where | Consent needed? | Every email must |
|---|---|---|
| United States (CAN-SPAM) | No. No B2B exception either: the law applies | Honest From and subject, say it is a commercial message, a valid postal address, a way to opt out, honored within 10 business days |
| France (CNIL) | No, if the email is about their job (accounting software to a CFO: yes; a holiday offer to a work address: no) | Say who is writing, say where you got their address and why you write, offer a simple free way to refuse |
| Rest of the EU (Belgium, the Netherlands and every other country not named here) | Depends on the country; some require consent for a named person even at a work address. Amber chip "{country}: rule to check" until the user confirms | Be relevant to their job, say who you are and where the address came from, easy opt-out, stop on request |
| Germany | Generally yes, even B2B (UWG section 7) | Do not cold email without a lawyer's view |
| United Kingdom (PECR) | No for companies; yes for sole traders and some partnerships | Say who you are, offer an opt-out |

## The footer

ONE footer, on email 1 only (the person was told who writes and where the address came from). Every
follow-up carries only the opt-out line: "Pas pertinent ? Répondez non." / "Not relevant? Reply no."
Exception: US recipients get the full US footer on every email (CAN-SPAM wants the address and the opt-out in
each commercial message). The footer is never counted in the word count and never names a provider.

English, EU recipients (they must be told where the address came from):

> {Name}, {Company}. I am writing about your work; I found your work address with a search tool.
> Not relevant? Reply "no" and I will not write again.

English, UK and elsewhere outside the EU and the US:

> {Name}, {Company}
> Not relevant? Reply "no" and I will not write again.

US recipients (CAN-SPAM adds the address and the commercial notice, on every email):

> {Name}, {Company}, {postal address}
> This is a commercial message. Not relevant? Reply "no" and I will not write again.

French (the CNIL asks who writes, why, and where the address came from):

> {Prénom Nom}, {Société}. Je vous écris pour votre travail ; adresse pro trouvée avec un outil de recherche.
> Pas pertinent ? Répondez « non » et je ne vous écrirai plus.

## Legitimate interest, in practice (EU)

The GDPR basis for using the data, separate from the consent question above. Regulators say it is not
automatic. It holds when:

1. The offer is relevant to the person's job.
2. They could reasonably expect a work email about it.
3. You tell them where their address came from and let them refuse easily.
4. You stop at once when they object, and keep a do-not-contact list.

## Found addresses (France, CNIL, October 2026)

- Finding a work email for prospecting is allowed on legitimate interest, case by case.
- Keep the address only if you actually contact the person within a reasonable time. Otherwise delete it:
  at hand-over, tell the user to delete the found addresses they do not email.
- If the person objects, never look up or use their address again.
- People who clearly hid their contact details cannot be expected to accept it: skip them.

## What BeReach does and what the user does

- BeReach finds the address and keeps it on the contact; the user decides to write and sends.
- The user is the sender: the footer, the opt-out and honoring it are theirs.
