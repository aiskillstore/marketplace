# Demo: how this skill actually works

A concrete run-through, so you can see the skill produce output instead of taking it on faith. Same request in, sharp plan out.

---

## User

> "I built a SaaS for finance automation. 0 customers. How do I get my first 10?"

---

## Step 1 — The gate (client #0 is you)

**Agent:** Before any marketing — are you using your own product?

**User:** No, not really.

**Agent:** Then that's client #0, and the marketing waits. The cheapest way to see the future is to solve your own problem with your own product. Come back when the answer is "yes — I use it daily."

*User goes and dogfoods. Comes back.*

---

## Step 2 — Competitors are the source of truth

**Agent:** Who is working the exact audience you want — and making money on it *right now*? Not the old names with no presence. The ones marketing hard today.

**User:** Three tools in my niche, all running ads.

**Agent:** Good. Those three are your benchmark. Let's see what they promise, how they price, and where their copy is weak.

---

## Step 3 — Steal the first client

**Agent:** Your first client is already someone else's customer. Three cold emails, three angles:

1. **Speed** — "we do in one click what [competitor] makes you configure for a week"
2. **Price** — "same core, half the cost of [competitor]"
3. **Pain** — "the one thing [competitor] users complain about — we fixed it"

---

## Step 4 — The McDonald's Burger

**Agent:** Your homepage says "finance automation." Flat. Here's the same product described the way McDonald's photographs a burger — the picture, not the plastic. Bold positioning, zero fiction:

> "Close your books before your coffee gets cold."

That's the same tool, photographed properly.

---

## Step 5 — By hand, then copy

**Agent:** Client #1 by hand — cold email, free. Clients 2–10 exactly how your three competitors already win them. Paid context ads only after the first 10 are proven.

---

## Result

A concrete, 3-month plan — specific competitors, three outbound angles, sharper positioning — instead of "write a blog post and do SEO."

That's the difference between a mindset and a bag of tactics.
