# Long-Work Evidence Coverage

Use this protocol when the work spans multiple files, chapters, episodes, versions, or more text than can be inspected reliably in one pass. It prevents a few vivid passages or search hits from standing in for the whole work.

## 1. Establish the Evidence Boundary

Before diagnosing, record an internal source manifest:

- source name or path;
- source authority and version, when known;
- expected structural units such as chapters, episodes, scenes, or appendices;
- units successfully read;
- units unread, inaccessible, truncated, duplicated, or ambiguous;
- supporting material that is context rather than canonical story text.

When sources conflict, preserve the conflict. Use an authority order supplied by the user or clearly established by the files; do not silently merge versions or promote notes into canon.

## 2. Read by Semantic Units

Read the complete relevant scope before making a work-wide diagnosis.

- Prefer chapters, episodes, scenes, or document sections over arbitrary token-sized excerpts.
- For oversized units, split them into labeled ranges and continue until the unit is complete.
- Use search to locate evidence, never as proof that unreturned material is absent.
- If an extraction or tool output is truncated, retry with smaller ranges. Do not continue as though the missing material was read.
- Keep enough overlap or context to preserve causal transitions across boundaries, but do not count duplicated overlap as additional coverage.

Do not repeatedly reread the opening while leaving later units unexamined.

## 3. Maintain a Coverage Ledger

For each structural unit, track at minimum:

- `READ`: complete, partial, failed, or not required;
- `ROLE`: setup, escalation, reversal, payoff, aftermath, or other relevant function;
- `STATE CHANGE`: what materially changes;
- `EVIDENCE`: concise anchors relevant to live hypotheses;
- `CONTRADICTIONS`: facts or actions that weaken those hypotheses;
- `OPEN QUESTIONS`: unresolved source or causality issues.

The ledger is working evidence. Keep it compact and do not expose it unless the user requests it or an audit requires it.

## 4. Match Coverage to Claim Scope

Evidence must span the scope of the claim.

- A scene claim may rely on that scene.
- A character-arc claim must cover the relevant appearances and transitions.
- A season-engine claim should normally test early setup, middle development, late payoff, and any claimed break point.
- A cross-file continuity claim must inspect every file that can establish or contradict it.

Two nearby anchors satisfy neither a season-wide claim nor a claim of repeated absence.

For each root diagnosis, keep separate:

- `Evidence`: what the covered text establishes;
- `Inference`: the most plausible explanation;
- `Diagnosis`: the proposed craft or structural problem;
- `Coverage`: which units support or limit the claim.

## 5. Handle Negative and Absence Claims

Only claim that something is absent when the relevant scope is fully covered.

If coverage is incomplete, use bounded language such as:

> No supporting action was found in the covered episodes; episodes 9-12 remain unread, so this is not yet a season-wide absence claim.

If a missing element would normally appear only in a specific unit, explain why that unit is the correct search boundary.

## 6. Completion Gate

Before finalizing:

1. compare expected units with read units;
2. inspect early, middle, and late evidence for work-wide claims;
3. verify at least one counterexample or weakening observation for each root diagnosis;
4. check that quoted or paraphrased evidence resolves to a source and structural unit;
5. narrow or defer claims affected by unread material;
6. disclose material coverage when it limits the recommendation.

Do not label the review complete merely because the output format is complete.
