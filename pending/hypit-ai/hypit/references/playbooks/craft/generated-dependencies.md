# Reference relationships

Every image request asks the painter for a complete picture. Connected references bring useful visual memory into that request: a person's appearance, a product's form, a place's atmosphere and geometry, a camera relationship, or a physical state. Some details need close continuity; others give the painter a world to reinterpret from a new viewpoint. Direct what the current picture carries forward and what it now shows. The relationships among images take the shape of those creative needs.

## Let one picture open another view

A podcast host image can establish a person, an encounter and the restaurant around them in one camera view. The image for the other host can use it as an anchor while changing several parts of the picture at once:

| Part of the new picture | How the new view uses the host image |
| --- | --- |
| Capture | The same phone-video photographic language. |
| Person | Cast the other host with their own appearance within this shared encounter. |
| Shot | The two views form a conversation: the new host sits on the other side, looks toward their partner, and receives the microphone from the corresponding side. |
| Setting | Both cameras inhabit the same Korean street-side restaurant and palette; this camera sees a different sector of its architecture, furniture and activity. |

The painter makes a full new view. The shared world and conversational geometry connect the pictures even as person, composition and visible background change. The symmetry belongs to the encounter; the second camera sees the part of the place available from its position.

The relationship matters more than copying a backdrop. A two-person encounter can carry who faces whom, where the microphone enters and which part of the place each camera sees. Separate call or reaction feeds can instead establish different rooms; their shared fact is who addresses or responds to whom, not a common wall.

The first host view can also meet a product image in another request. In that picture, the host and much of the camera view continue, while her hands, the phone and its displayed product change. One reference can support very different kinds of variation because each new picture has its own intention. The [podcast and interview image examples](examples/conversation-images.md) show the actual prompts and inputs behind these relationships.

## Give each new picture the references it needs

A useful first camera image can establish person and place together. It may show one person, several people or the whole encounter. When another view needs that world, carry its atmosphere, palette, materials and spatial relationships into the view the new camera should see. Several video requests can then use the resulting images.

A new location can begin with a camera image that already places the people in that setting. Their established views can supply identity or styling; a product or continuing prop can supply its form and state. The new picture establishes the location and composition together, while the prior images carry the facts that persist across the move. A character reference can also carry identity directly into video direction when the new place does not need its own key image.

If one character leaves a living-room argument holding a phone, a hallway view can inherit the person's identity and the phone's relevant state when that moment merits an image. The furniture and camera angle of the living room have finished their job. An intentional change of clothes, time or setting similarly changes which facts the next picture needs to carry. A view may grow from the previous story view when its changing state matters, or branch from a character reference when earlier settings have nothing to contribute.

A portrait may contribute identity while clothes, action and place change. A product-holding variation may carry nearly the whole view while changing the hands and object. When there is no person, the picture can still draw on a product, place, graphic or composition reference. Choose the useful reference by the relationship the next picture needs. Keeping close to the image that actually knows that relationship also keeps incidental details from unrelated intermediate scenes from accumulating.

An existing image can serve the intended view directly. When the work asks for another picture, connect the images that carry its needed facts and describe their roles in the prompt. With several references, “reference 2” means the second image connected to this request; the actual image input gives that phrase its meaning. A request whose references already establish much of the picture may need only a short direction for the change. The selected image package owns the exact reference-input syntax.

Keep useful prompts and source images with the project when later views may need the same relationship. [Image direction](image-direction.md) owns the picture's casting, framing, palette and capture language; [Run Candidates](../../production/authoring.md#reuse-produced-work-explicitly) explain reuse of completed Outputs.

## Carry the shared world into motion

The image graph records visual facts to carry forward, not the video work's shot order or request boundaries. One camera image may guide a passage with several views or multiple generated clips in the same world; several images may contribute different facts to one request. A video excerpt can carry performed action or camera behavior that a still cannot show; [video direction](video-direction.md#let-footage-carry-temporal-behavior-that-matters) owns those input choices and the requested action. [Voice and performance](voice-and-performance.md) and [B-roll](b-roll.md) own the resulting footage's roles in the work.
