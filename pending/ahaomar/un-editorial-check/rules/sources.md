# Sources

Checked on **24 September 2026**.

1. [UN Geneva web style guide](https://www.ungeneva.org/en/styleguide): “In line with the United Nations Editorial Manual, the website uses British English unless otherwise declared.”
2. [United Nations Editorial Manual Online](https://www.un.org/dgacm/en/content/editorial-manual): the Numbers section records, “Both the percentage sign (%) and ‘per cent’ may be used in running text, as long as the document is internally consistent. The percentage sign should not be used in resolutions.” The checker therefore keeps ordinary prose silent and documents resolution context as a limitation.
3. *Concise Oxford English Dictionary*, 12th edition: spelling authority; use the first-listed form under the UN framework.

These sources establish the editorial baseline. They do not establish that a factual claim is true, current or proportionate. Source factual prose to the original dataset or report and include its date. If a report is quoted in a standfirst or post, cite it in the page footer with a link.

Some web style guidance can change. Re-check the manual before treating this package as a substitute for current institutional guidance.

## Claim knowledge base

Each `diplomacy.claims` entry in `config/profiles/un-v1.json` carries its own citation:

1. Jammu and Kashmir — United Nations Security Council resolution 47 (1948) on the question of Jammu and Kashmir.
2. Taiwan — United Nations General Assembly resolution 2758 (1971) and the UN Terminology Bulletin.
3. Hong Kong — Sino-British Joint Declaration on the Question of Hong Kong, registered with the United Nations (1984).
4. Crimea — United Nations General Assembly resolution 68/262 (2014) on the territorial integrity of Ukraine.

These citations make each entry reviewable; they do not adjudicate the underlying dispute. Re-check every entry against the current status of its question before relying on it, and record the check date in the entry itself.
