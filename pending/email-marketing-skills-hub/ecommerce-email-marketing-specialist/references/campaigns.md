# Campaigns and copy

## Decide the campaign before writing it

Capture: business goal; recipient eligibility/exclusions; verified product/category; approved offer and terms; desired action and landing URL; inventory/readiness; send date with time zone; overlap with other messages; primary KPI and guardrails. If audience size, margin or seasonality is unknown, mark it unknown rather than inventing a calendar justified by fake data.

Build a calendar around distinct buyer needs: discovery/education, new products, useful category guidance, credible customer proof, replenishment and occasional promotions. Avoid defaulting to a daily discount schedule. Start with the store's established cadence and adjust using outcomes and fatigue, not a generic frequency rule.

For each calendar row include date/time zone, segment, purpose, approved offer or none, subject angle, destination, exclusions, owner, dependency and KPI. Identify automation collisions. Define which message takes precedence; do not silently pause live flows.

## Copy packet

- Subject: accurate, specific promise. Provide variants when requested or tied to a test.
- Preheader: adds information instead of repeating the subject.
- Body: one main idea, scannable benefits supported by source facts, one primary action.
- CTA: descriptive action and verified destination, plus plain-text alternative where needed.
- Personalization: exact provider token only after verifying syntax; otherwise semantic placeholder plus fallback. Never expose raw merge tags in a launch-ready claim.
- Footer: verified business identity and required contact/address details; working unsubscribe/preferences mechanism. Do not invent a business address.
- Terms: approved discount scope, minimum, stacking, exclusions, expiry with time zone, and fulfillment limits.

Avoid fake reply prefixes, invented scarcity, guilt/shaming, fake testimonials and resetting countdowns. Product category can require additional claim review; do not improvise health or guaranteed-result claims.

## Useful campaign variants

**New collection:** relevant category interest, proof of what's new, exclusions for recent purchasers if redundant, category landing page.

**Promotion:** validate margin and inventory first; separate first-time and repeat buyers if eligibility differs; suppress redeemed customers from reminders; do not issue a stronger coupon by default.

**Education:** resolve a real purchase objection; link to accurate product guidance; useful even without a discount.

**Win-back campaign:** use the store's normal repurchase interval and prior engagement, not a universal 90-day definition. Respect suppression and avoid repeated attempts to uninterested contacts.

## Review criteria

Can the reader tell who is writing, why it matters, what to do, and what conditions apply? Does the landing page fulfill the promise? Is the mobile hierarchy clear? Are offer and segment consistent? Is the language the brand's rather than generic marketing hype?
