# Security-service controls and sources

Primary sources checked 2026-11-04. Check the relevant jurisdiction and exact service before making regulated claims.

- [NIST SP 800-216](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/216/final) provides a federal vulnerability-disclosure framework and handling/communication guidance. Use it as a reference for clear reporting ownership, not as authorization to test a private prospect.
- [CISA's public disclosure-policy template](https://github.com/cisagov/cyber.dhs.gov/blob/master/pages/20-01-vdp-template.md) illustrates explicitly bounded authorization. A public VDP covers only its stated systems/methods; marketing interest creates no equivalent authorization.
- [UK SIA licensing guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-you-need-an-sia-licence) distinguishes activities and license needs. Do not generalize UK rules to another country, or individual licensing to organization-wide approval. Verify current local licensing for guards, alarm installation, monitoring and investigations as applicable.

Do not invent certification, penetration-testing authority, service-level agreements, insurance coverage or government endorsement. Require evidence for technical comparisons and dated proof of service scope. Publishing exploit details or operational weaknesses requires a separate coordinated disclosure decision, not ordinary marketing approval.

Approval packet: service and region, target audience, exact claims/evidence, customer permission and redactions, intake boundaries, data destinations, staff/licensing representations, response expectations, owner and emergency routing. Keep an approved campaign separate from any contract, security access or technical assessment authorization.
