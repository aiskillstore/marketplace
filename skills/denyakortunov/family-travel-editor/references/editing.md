# Editing principles

Every shot needs a role: orient, reveal, develop an action, show a meaningful detail, convey beauty/atmosphere/scale or give a reaction. A shot need not contain speech or a new factual claim. Judge how adjacent shots develop the scene together. A new angle, crop or source file alone does not justify repeating the same content. End after the action reads and before technical camera searching; preserve purposeful reframing. Judge camera movement in motion, not from one frame.

Maintain direction, eye line and action phase when appropriate; use a change in scale or pace for a reason. Do not impose a montage rhythm, music cue or duration quota on every scene. Dialogue and reactions can take priority over a musical beat. Keep alternate takes and older versions.

Voiceover should not cover family conversation. Keep the camera atmosphere and separate speech/music/effects tracks. Under dialogue, choose a stable bed rather than pumping every pause. Check newly introduced music for competing voices across the used section. Effects and musical returns should reflect the action, with source/licensing notes.

Inserts ripple all linked material unless a replacement was requested. Protect later titles, maps, sound effects and sync. Compare all affected tracks, not only the main video track. A rendered preview is not a substitute for an editable project. Label reduced-resolution review copies; export a final master from the available original resolutions.
