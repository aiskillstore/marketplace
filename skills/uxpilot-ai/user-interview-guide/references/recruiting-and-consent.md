# Recruiting, screening, consent, and privacy

## Recruit from behavior

Choose participants who can supply the evidence the decision needs. Start with a recent qualifying action, not a persona label.

Define:

- behavior: what they personally did;
- timeframe: recent enough for useful recall;
- context: product status, team role, workflow, or decision involvement;
- variation: meaningful differences the team needs to understand;
- exclusions: conflicts, professional participants, insiders, or missing authority;
- access needs: accommodations required for equitable participation.

Example:

> Include PMs who personally created a user flow for a live product decision in the last 30 days. Include both people who used AI and people who did not. Exclude people who only reviewed someone else's flow.

## Write non-leading screeners

- Ask about behavior before opinions.
- Use precise, mutually exclusive answer ranges where needed.
- Include plausible distractors so the desired answer is not obvious.
- Do not reveal the product concept or accept-answer logic.
- Keep the screener short enough that qualified people finish it.
- Verify critical behavior again at the beginning of the session.

Bad:

> Do you struggle with creating user flows manually?

Better:

> Which of these activities have you personally done for a product decision during the last 30 days? [randomized list]

Then ask when the most recent relevant activity occurred and what tools were used.

## Small qualitative rounds

Recruit in small rounds that allow the guide and participant criteria to improve. Four to eight interviews is a common practical starting range, not a statistical rule. Do not claim representativeness or saturation automatically. Continue, narrow, or change method based on the decision, heterogeneity, evidence quality, and emerging gaps.

## Consent essentials

Before participation, explain truthfully:

- who is conducting the research;
- its purpose in understandable terms;
- what the session involves and how long it lasts;
- whether audio, video, screen, notes, or artifacts will be captured;
- who will access the material and how it may be used;
- whether quotations or clips may be shared;
- how long identifiable data will be retained, if known;
- that participation is voluntary and questions may be skipped;
- how to withdraw within the actual policy and practical limits;
- the incentive and any conditions.

Recording consent is explicit, not implied by joining a call. Research consent is not the same as marketing consent.

## Privacy and sensitive research

- Collect only data needed for the research decision.
- Avoid putting identifiable participant data in widely shared notes.
- Provide a safe path to redact or avoid confidential artifacts.
- Do not infer legal compliance from this guide; follow the organization's policies and applicable law.
- Escalate research involving children, health, finances, trauma, vulnerable populations, regulated data, or material risk to qualified research, privacy, legal, or ethics support.

## Recruitment output

Return:

1. inclusion criteria;
2. exclusion criteria;
3. useful variation;
4. 3 to 6 screener questions;
5. consent and accessibility considerations;
6. recruitment risks that could bias the study.
