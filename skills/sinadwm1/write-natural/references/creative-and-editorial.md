# Creative range, cultural fit and editorial agency

Use this module for stories, essays, voice-sensitive edits, localization, and sets of articles, captions, messages or campaign variants. For factual and task-level release checks, use [quality-gates.md](quality-gates.md).

## Evidence boundary

- **R03:** An English microfiction experiment found gains in some individual story ratings alongside greater similarity among assisted stories; professional writers and unrestricted long-form work were not tested.
- **R04:** English writing tasks with Indian and US participants showed cultural convergence under autocomplete assistance; this was not a Persian-language study.
- **R06:** A preprint compared computational rewrites of existing English essays. Structural diversity effects varied by dimension and prompt; actual student interaction and cognition were not measured.
- **R07:** Excess vocabulary in biomedical English supports population-level analysis, not reliable identification of an individual abstract's authorship.

The following editorial procedures and all examples are original synthesis. They are practical design choices, not proven remedies for homogenization, validated creativity tests, or ways to guarantee a detector result.

## XC-01: Establish what belongs to this author

For revision, use the supplied draft and any authorized samples to identify stable traits: stance, degree of directness, level of formality, idiom, sentence movement, use of detail and relation to the reader. Separate these traits from facts, copyrighted passages, and errors in the sample. Preserve a distinctive phrase if it does useful work. Do not replace an author's position with a smoother conventional opinion.

For new writing, infer a suitable register from the artifact and audience. If a consequential choice cannot be inferred, ask one focused question; otherwise state a modest assumption and proceed. Do not invent an author biography, emotional history, insider knowledge, or a local identity to create a “human” voice.

**Review:** Compare the first and final versions for altered stance, flattened emotional intensity, replaced idioms and deleted concrete details. Restore anything whose removal merely makes the prose resemble a generic model answer.

**Exception:** The user may authorize a new voice, adaptation or substantial restructuring. Make the requested change while preserving factual constraints, and avoid presenting fictional experience as the user's own.

## XC-02: Preserve cultural specificity without assigning a stereotype

Keep relevant supplied names, places, foods, rituals, social relationships and locally meaningful terms. Preserve the intended level of respect and directness. Check whether a suggestion merely makes the text conform to a default US business voice. Do not infer one required style for everyone who writes Persian or English.

In Persian, choose formal, conversational, academic or literary usage from the deliverable's purpose, audience and author signals. “Natural” does not mean slang. In English, preserve the requested variety, including consistent spelling and idiom. In translation, retain the original argument, social function and evidential limits; choose target-language phrasing rather than mechanically transferring word order.

**Review:** Identify any cultural detail removed, generalized or newly introduced. Keep a removal only when it serves the brief, intelligibility or necessary compression. Explain a necessary local term briefly for an unfamiliar audience instead of replacing the underlying detail with a generic celebration or lifestyle description.

**Exception:** Localization can deliberately change examples, dates or conventions. Change factual or identity-bearing content only when the brief authorizes it and the replacement is verified or clearly fictional. Do not add decorative cultural references simply to signal authenticity.

## XC-03: Create alternatives that differ in their underlying idea

When the user requests alternatives or a collection, define the useful variation before drafting: reader problem, central claim, evidence angle, narrative perspective, causal sequence, emotional movement, ending, or action requested. Select only axes that suit the task. Draft variants from different briefs or outlines, rather than repeatedly paraphrasing the first output.

A batch review should compare the actual texts across these axes:

| Axis | Review question | Useful repair |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Are several entries making the same point? | Use a different supported question or consequence |
| Evidence | Does every entry lean on the same example? | Choose another available case or mark the evidence gap |
| Structure | Do openings, turns and endings follow one template? | Reorder according to a different reader need or story event |
| Perspective | Does every voice see and value the same things? | Develop a different defensible viewpoint or character motive |
| Expression | Are signature phrases crowding out substance? | Replace empty phrasing with relevant detail already supported |

Keep terminology, claims, brand facts and required disclosures stable. Meaningful variety is compatible with repeated technical terms. Do not inject random synonyms, typos, eccentric punctuation, unsupported anecdotes or arbitrary sentence-length quotas. Differences should earn their place through the task.

**Exception:** A consistent series, legal notice, procedure or product catalogue may need a shared structure and vocabulary. Preserve that consistency; vary only where different content or user needs justify it. Batch similarity alone is not a defect.

## XC-04: Revise the identified weakness, then inspect the whole

Choose a targeted editing instruction with a reason: clarify the causal step; make the character's decision legible; connect the example to the claim; remove a duplicated argument; or adjust register for a named audience. Preserve the author's valid perspective and organization unless they are the diagnosed problem.

After a targeted rewrite, inspect the revised text for new unsupported content, forced counterarguments, generic transitions and unwanted template structure. A more specific prompt can still damage another dimension. Do not require a counterargument in a practical instruction, a twist in every story, or an equal balance between positions with unequal evidence.

**Review:** Compare the changed passage with the original in context. Use separate criteria for coherence, originality, voice and correctness. If the passage is already effective, leave it alone. The quality module governs priority and stopping.

**Exception:** A brainstorming request can explore speculative positions or fictional events. Keep that creative frame clear and do not carry the inventions into factual copy unnoticed.

## XC-05: Treat lexical patterns as editorial cues

A word-frequency pattern can motivate a contextual question: is this word precise, informative and suited to the author? It does not establish authorship. Examine clusters of vague, inflated or repeated language for what they fail to communicate; revise the actual defect.

Preserve a necessary term even if it appears on an “AI words” list. Do not assign a probability of AI authorship from vocabulary, translate English marker lists into Persian and call them validated, or promise that removing words makes writing undetectable. This module makes no watermark or provenance changes.

**Exception:** If the user requests corpus research, distinguish group statistics from individual classification, document language and sampling limits, and use a suitable analysis method. A small batch review is editorial judgment, not an estimate of population AI usage.

## Original examples: meaning-led alternatives and cultural detail

The following lines are invented examples. Facts specified inside each hypothetical brief are premises for the example, not verified product or historical claims.

**English, three variants from three reader needs**

Hypothetical brief: a notes app keeps notes on the device, works offline and can export plain text. The audience already knows what it does.

- Offline access: “Keep writing when the train loses signal.”
- Portability: “Export your notes as plain text whenever you need them elsewhere.”
- Storage choice: “Keep your notes on this device.”

These address distinct concerns. Three synonyms for “effortless productivity” would add little range. Do not strengthen the last line into a security guarantee; on-device storage alone does not establish security.

**Persian, preserving one fictional family's actual detail**

Hypothetical story note: «مادربزرگ هر سال کاسهٔ مسی را کنار سبزه می‌گذاشت.»

Flattened: «خانواده با شادی و صمیمیت، سنت‌های زیبای نوروز را گرامی می‌داشتند.»

Detail-preserving: «مادربزرگ کاسهٔ مسی را کنار سبزه گذاشت؛ همان‌جا که هر سال می‌گذاشت.»

Keep the detail when it contributes to memory, setting or character. Do not invent a universal custom around the bowl or add other ritual objects without a narrative reason.

**Persian, natural formality without losing the request**

Administrative context, specified deadline: «لطفاً نسخهٔ اصلاح‌شده را تا سه‌شنبه ارسال کنید تا بررسی نهایی انجام شود.»

Familiar colleague, conversational channel: «می‌تونی نسخهٔ اصلاح‌شده رو تا سه‌شنبه بفرستی؟ بعدش بررسی نهایی رو انجام می‌دیم.»

Choose according to the relationship and channel. Do not turn the first into the second merely because the user asked for “human” writing. Keep the deadline and the planned next step in either register.
