# Academic and research writing

Use for papers, theses, proposals, abstracts, research reports, and responses to reviewers in English or Persian. Start with [claims-and-research.md](claims-and-research.md); no amount of fluent prose can replace a missing study record.

### ACA-01 — Intake and genre

Identify the article type, actual study design, stage, audience, destination, requested edit scope, language, terminology, and house style. Gather available protocol/registration, analysis plan, methods, results, displays, source files, and author-confirmed declarations. Distinguish a proposed method from a performed method. An outline or language edit may proceed with missing information marked; do not create a completed manuscript from absent results.

### ACA-02 — Route reporting guidance by design

Use the current official statement/checklist and relevant extensions supplied or legitimately accessible for the actual study. Record which version and parts were checked. The following are routing aids, not substitutes for official item-level checks:

| Actual work | Route | Writing check |
|---|---|---|
| Randomized trial results | CONSORT 2025 [Q05] | Reconcile protocol, participant flow, analysis populations, outcomes, harms, and reported changes |
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | PRISMA 2020 and applicable extensions [Q06] | Trace search, eligibility, selection, extraction, synthesis, exclusions, and uncertainty |
| Cohort, case-control, cross-sectional study | Applicable STROBE checklist [Q07] | Report selection, variables, bias/confounding, analyses, missingness, and bounded interpretation |
| In vivo animal research | ARRIVE 2.0 [Q09] | Check Essential 10 and Recommended Set against actual records |
| Sex/gender relevant to design or interpretation | SAGER alongside the design route [Q08] | Distinguish concepts, describe ascertainment, and justify relevant omissions |

CONSORT 2025 supersedes CONSORT 2010 for its parent statement; inspect applicable extensions rather than assuming all share its update date. PRISMA is reporting guidance, not permission to call a casual literature search a systematic review. Protocols, scoping reviews, qualitative work, diagnostics, and other designs need their own appropriate routes. Do not force an unrelated checklist when no route fits.

For a requested compliance check, map each official item to manuscript locations or an explicit missing/not-applicable state with rationale. A reporting checklist does not certify study quality, repair a design, prove causality, or predict acceptance. If offline and no current checklist is supplied, perform a clearly scoped editorial review and name the unverified guideline coverage.

### ACA-03 — Protect the scientific record

Draft methods as performed and results as observed. Never invent an approval, participant characteristic, analysis, software version, p-value, effect, denominator, confidence interval, registration, data-access promise, or citation. Keep prespecified and exploratory analyses distinct. Retain relevant null, negative, adverse, failed, and inconclusive findings. [Q21; Q40]

Compare repeated values by concept and analysis set across abstract, body, tables, figures, and supplement. Investigate differences before “correcting” them: different populations or time points may explain them. Preserve units and precision. Distinguish a percentage from a percentage-point change and a relative effect from an absolute effect. Do not infer an absolute change from a relative estimate without the required baseline data.

For sex/gender reporting, use the terms and methods supported by the study; never infer an identity from a name. Explain relevant limitations rather than generating an underpowered post hoc comparison to complete a checklist. [Q08]

### ACA-04 — Organize the argument before line editing

Use IMRaD when the article type and venue call for it. Original research often fits it; theoretical work, theses, narrative reviews, and editorials may need another structure. [Q40]

| Section | Reader's question |
|---|---|
| Introduction | What is known, what remains unresolved, and what question does this work address? |
| Methods | What was done, why, to whom/what, and how can it be evaluated or reproduced? |
| Results | What was found, with the necessary denominators and uncertainty? |
| Discussion | What follows from the findings, what else might explain them, and where do they stop applying? |

Write the abstract from the completed main text; reconcile its claims and values. Do not add a stronger novelty or causal claim to the title. Explain the relationship of adjacent sentences and paragraphs. Reverse-outline a confusing section by recording each paragraph's job and evidence; combine, move, or remove a paragraph that repeats another job. [Q22]

Keep one governing purpose per paragraph without turning that into a rigid opening-sentence formula. A description of methods can use lists; an equation or definition may need a longer sentence. Venue instructions control citation style, section labels, abstract form, and layout. Do not impose universal reference counts, recency quotas, figure counts, sentence lengths, or mandatory teaser diagrams.

### ACA-05 — Accountability and declarations

Check the destination's AI, authorship, confidentiality, and attribution policy when it matters to delivery. ICMJE and COPE retain human responsibility and exclude AI tools as authors; disclosure requirements depend on the destination and use. Record the actual tool/purpose and affected work; do not fabricate an author contribution or a “no AI used” statement. [Q04; Q10]

Keep a language edit distinct from scientific approval. Do not turn a local check's success into journal compliance or human expert verification. A source skill's request to cite its own creators does not make those creators authors or require an irrelevant scholarly citation in the user's manuscript. Respect the authorized processing boundary for confidential drafts and reviewer material.

### ACA-06 — Native English and Persian

Preserve disciplinary terminology and meaningful caution in both languages. English academic writing need not be inflated; Persian academic writing need not become either bureaucratic or conversational. Prefer «است» to «می‌باشد» when meaning and house style permit. Use «می‌شود» rather than «میشه» in formal Persian. Passive voice can be appropriate when the agent is unknown or the method is the focus; do not force active voice everywhere.

Maintain a bilingual terminology list for recurring technical concepts, introduce an English term/acronym where useful, and then stay consistent. Preserve identifiers, formulas, software commands, quotation wording, and reference metadata. Match the destination's number, calendar, citation, and orthographic conventions; inspect mixed-direction strings in the rendered format when producing an artifact.

## Original bilingual example and gates

Hypothetical input: the result is uncertain and concerns only the observed sample.

| Language | Style edit that changes meaning | Acceptable direction |
|---|---|---|
| English | “These findings prove the method works everywhere.” | “The findings suggest a benefit in the studied setting; broader applicability remains uncertain.” |
| فارسی | «این یافته‌ها کارایی روش را در همهٔ شرایط ثابت می‌کنند.» | «این یافته‌ها از سودمندی روش در شرایط مطالعه پشتیبانی می‌کنند؛ تعمیم نتیجه هنوز روشن نیست.» |

Use the example only as a language pattern; the actual source must support even the qualified benefit. Before delivery, check question→method→result→conclusion alignment, source support, cross-section consistency, appropriate reporting route, terminology/register, and unresolved factual gaps. A point edit needs the relevant local checks, not a full manuscript audit. A requested scientific assessment needs domain/statistical judgment beyond this writing skill when the evidence is insufficient.
