# Web articles, educational pages, and SEO

Use for search-facing articles, product information, reviews, explainers, and tutorials in Persian or English. Pair factual work with [claims-and-research.md](claims-and-research.md), and commercial persuasion with [copy-and-sales.md](copy-and-sales.md).

### WEB-01 — Define the reader's task and the page's contribution

Identify the intended reader, question, journey stage, page type, language/region, evidence available, and desired next step. Infer a reasonable brief when stakes are low; ask only for missing information that changes the answer. Write a one-sentence completion condition: what should the reader understand, decide, or do after the page?

Add a defensible contribution: an explanation that resolves confusion, a useful comparison, a worked example, original analysis, or documented experience. Name which contribution the source material permits. Rewording other pages does not by itself create reader value. [Q01]

Plan around the question rather than a keyword quota. Give the useful answer early when the reader needs a decision or instruction. Keep necessary assumptions, prerequisites, limitations, and troubleshooting. Use descriptive headings and meaningful links so readers can find the needed information. Concision and scanning are useful design considerations; an older usability study's percentages are not modern conversion promises. [Q24]

### WEB-02 — Verify the entire promise

Check factual claims in the body, heading, title, description, caption, alt text, and proposed structured data. A correct article with an exaggerated title still misleads. Generated content needs editorial review and real added value; generation method alone does not establish search quality. Do not create many low-value pages to manipulate rankings or disguise copied material through synonym changes or translation. [Q02; Q03]

Do not promise a ranking, featured placement, traffic increase, or detector result. No universal minimum word count, keyword density, citation count, or “E-E-A-T score” is supported here. Use real author/reviewer credentials and actual evidence of experience where relevant; never manufacture a biography, test, visit, or expert endorsement. [Q01]

### WEB-03 — Write accurate, distinct titles

Make the title specific to this page, descriptive, and concise enough to scan. Align its promise with the main heading and content without making every string identical. Avoid repeated boilerplate, keyword stuffing, unsupported superlatives, and a year that implies an update that did not occur. Google can generate a different title link, and display truncation depends on width; a fixed character count is a drafting heuristic, not an official rule. Match the page's main language and writing system. [Q31]

### WEB-04 — Reviews need attributable grounds

Choose explicit decision criteria and relevant alternatives. Separate manufacturer specifications, reported customer experiences, editorial inference, and the reviewer's own tests. State the conditions and limits of any measurement. Explain suitability and tradeoffs for a use case. A “best” recommendation needs supporting grounds, not a decorative ranking. Never write “we tested” unless the test actually occurred. [Q30]

### WEB-05 — Keep an audit tied to what was observed

For each finding record affected page → observation → evidence/inspection method → reader or search consequence → proposed fix. Distinguish a content review from a technical site audit. Static text extraction cannot establish everything about rendered markup or interaction. Do not claim “no schema exists” from an extraction that may have removed scripts. [Q19]

Use current official documentation for technical recommendations. The public audit skill reviewed here still lists the retired Mobile-Friendly Test; its inclusion does not make the tool current. Google's retirement notice supersedes that recommendation. [Q35] Do not inherit deterministic title-length, keyword-placement, or traffic-causation rules from a public skill merely because other parts are useful.

### WEB-06 — Native Persian and English

Use actual audience/search evidence when available; label modeled search intent as a hypothesis. Persian pages need natural Persian wording, consistent register, correct ی/ک and نیم‌فاصله, readable mixed-direction terms, and locally appropriate units/currency. Do not stuff every spelling or spacing variant into prose. Localize examples and commercial conditions only from known facts; translation does not authorize invented local availability or pricing.

For tutorials, preserve exact commands and identifiers in either language. Clearly distinguish tested instructions from instructions drafted from documentation. Include prerequisites, actions, expected outcome, and recovery when they help the reader complete the task. Do not claim to have run a command merely because it appears in a source skill. [Q20]

## Original paired example

Hypothetical brief: explain how to compare two storage plans; no product testing or ranking data is supplied.

| Language | Unsupported title | Useful title direction |
|---|---|---|
| English | “The world's best storage plan, guaranteed” | “How to compare storage plans by capacity, recovery, and cost” |
| فارسی | «بهترین سرویس ذخیره‌سازی جهان، با تضمین» | «مقایسهٔ طرح‌های ذخیره‌سازی بر اساس ظرفیت، بازیابی و هزینه» |

The revised title commits the page to supplying those comparisons. Remove a criterion if the necessary information is unavailable, or state the limitation.

## Delivery gates and exceptions

Check that the page answers its question, delivers its title promise, adds useful information, supports claims, preserves limits, and gives the reader a clear route through the text. An article can be long when the task needs detail. A creative feature or essay can delay its conclusion deliberately. A point edit should not grow into an unsolicited site audit. Offline work can improve content and flag source gaps; it cannot certify current rankings, policies, indexing, or technical behavior.
