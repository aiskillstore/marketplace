# The seven expressive tactics

From "Start building with Material 3 Expressive" (m3.material.io/blog/building-with-m3-expressive).

These emerged as Google products adopted the expressive features. **Each tactic is one axis along
which you can make components, layouts, and products more expressive.** They are guidance, not law —
M3 Expressive deliberately gives makers more flexibility than before, and Material encourages playing
with these elements.

The underlying purpose of every tactic is the same: **guide the viewer's attention to the most
important elements of the screen.** A change that doesn't do that isn't expressive, it's noise.

---

## 1. Use a variety of shapes

Shape is a communication tool. At the base level of visual design, the shapes of components,
containers, and content **set the tone at first glance**.

- Combining shapes and corner radii creates **visual tension or cohesion**, and directs focus.
- Use a combination of **classic and abstract** shapes to create unique silhouettes or groupings.
- Use the shape library and the new corner-radii options to **mix round and square** shapes for
  tension and visual contrast.
- **Break from the surrounding shape style to draw attention to a particular element.**

Failure mode: **smaller shapes make essential actions look less important.**

## 2. Apply rich and nuanced colors

The dynamic color system already offers primary, secondary, and tertiary roles and surfaces.

- **Mix these colors** for key components or visual elements to emphasize the screen's main takeaway.
- **Create visual hierarchy with surface tones.**
- Use contrast between primary, secondary, and tertiary roles to **prioritize actions and simplify
  navigation.**

Failure mode: **without contrast, elements blend together.**

## 3. Guide attention with typography

- Use **emphasized text styles** to draw attention to important UI elements — headlines and actions.
- Create **editorial-like moments** by emphasizing typography.
- **Heavier weights, larger sizes, color, and spacing** direct attention and make key information
  more engaging.
- Using **additional type styles** from the Material type scale creates appropriate hierarchy *within*
  and *between* blocks of content.

## 4. Contain content for emphasis

- Organize content into **logical groupings or containers**.
- Give the most important content, tasks, or actions visual prominence through **ample space and the
  brightest surface mapping**.
- Consider **size, spacing, rhythm, similarity, or other grouping principles** to make important
  elements more distinct.

Failure mode: **ungrouped information blends together.**

## 5. Add fluid and natural motion

- Make interactions feel alive and spirited through **shape morph or surface effects**.
- Apply the **expressive motion springs** or custom micro animations.

## 6. Leverage component flexibility

- **UI should adapt to the user context.**
- **Shift components or controls depending on the environment** to make completing tasks easier.
- Adapt content to **foldable and large screens** through custom tweaks or by applying **canonical
  layouts**.

## 7. Combine tactics to create hero moments

Hero moments use **multiple** expressive tactics at once to break from predictable or uniformly
applied design. They make a stand-alone statement, or frame essential information in a fresh,
editorial way. A hero moment is a **focusing mechanism** — invest your time in making the most
critical interactions sing.

Hero moments are **brief, delightful, surprising, and unexpected.**

> **Stick to one or two hero moments in your product. Too many are overwhelming or distracting.**

To identify your hero moment, ask:

- **Is this interaction emotionally impactful?** Can a design choice highlight an emotional reaction
  or emphasize familiarity?
- **Is this a key interaction in your product?** Can a detail or flow be emphasized for clarity —
  making an important button dominant, or emphasizing key information?

## What M3 Expressive is (and isn't)

An **evolution of Material 3** — a set of new features, updated components, and design tactics for
creating emotionally impactful UX.

- **It is not a new version.** M3 is not deprecated. **This isn't "M4."**
- Expressive interfaces have **emotional impact**, fostering connection by evoking a feeling or mood
  through visual design and interaction.
- The **Usability** guidelines describe the tactics as working through "**containment, size, shape,
  color, and typography**" — phrased as *"tactics like…"*, so it is illustrative, not a closed list.
  It does not mention motion, whereas the seven tactics on this page clearly do. Don't quote either
  list as *the* definitive set of five or six things; the tactics below are the authoritative
  enumeration.

## The research

M3 Expressive is Material's **most researched update since the system launched in 2014**:
**46 studies with more than 18,000 participants**. Takeaways:

- Expressive designs are **preferred by people of all ages**.
- They consistently score higher on **playfulness, energy, creativity, and friendliness**.
- Users are **more likely to switch** to products using M3 Expressive components and techniques.
- Expressive designs are **easier to use** — participants spotted key UI elements **up to four times
  faster** in expressive screens.

That last finding is the argument that matters: expressiveness here is a **usability** mechanism, not
a decorative one. Makers were asking for better hierarchy, more useful interfaces, and more personal
styles that connect emotionally.

## What shipped in the update

**14 new or updated components** with more configuration capabilities, shape options, emphasized
text, and other expressive updates:

App bars · **Button groups (new)** · Common buttons · Extended FAB · **FAB menu (new)** · FABs ·
Icon buttons · **Loading indicator (new)** · Navigation bar · Navigation rail · Progress indicators ·
Sliders · **Split button (new)** · **Toolbars (new)**

**Styles:** motion-physics system (spatial + effects springs) · visually emphasized typography ·
expanded shape library (35 shapes + shape morph) · vibrant color schemes.
