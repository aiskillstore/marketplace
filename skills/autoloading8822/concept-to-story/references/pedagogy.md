# Learning pace and continuity

These are workflow choices, not a claim that this repository has demonstrated a retention benefit.

## Maps before detail

For a chapter, select normally 3–7 substantive concepts. Explain dependencies: “B uses A because …”. Mark parallel explanations explicitly; do not force everything into a causal chain. Begin with a concept needed by the rest.

For a whole book, first produce only a **Book Map** with normally 5–12 major nodes, the central question, argument path, and relationships. If the document supports fewer nodes, use fewer. A map based on partial inspection must distinguish verified claims from tentative headings and unread regions. Ask for a starting preference or suggest one; wait before detailed stories.

## Interactive lesson loop

1. Teach one source-grounded concept and end on one scenario-based recall question.
2. Wait. Give no answer, including in collapsed Markdown or a “hint” that reveals it.
3. After the learner responds, identify what they got right and any specific error against the source. A near miss calls for one focused follow-up, not three new questions.
4. Once they correctly apply the mechanism and a relevant boundary, ask one transfer question in a new setting. Wait again.
5. On “continue,” advance within the planned batch. If they skipped a pending question, mark it untested and proceed without shaming, blocking, or inventing success.

An explicit answer request permits an explanation; record it as an answer shown, not independent mastery. A request to stay on the previous concept changes the explanation or offers a new scene; it does not move ahead just because the word “continue” appeared.

## Concept linking

Connect a new concept to an earlier one only when the relationship is useful and supported. Identify whether it depends on, contrasts with, or offers a parallel explanation to the earlier concept. For example, reciprocity concerns patterns of help in both directions; special obligations concern reasons that arise from a relationship. Neither implies exact scorekeeping or unlimited access.

## Review and next-day acceptance

When the learner returns for review, show only the anchor first and ask them to reconstruct the mechanism. In a later turn, test a different situation and a boundary. Do not present the entire explanation before asking them to retrieve it.

Useful observations are: unaided retrieval, prompted retrieval, confusion between concepts, and correct transfer. A single immediate answer is not evidence of long-term memory. Next-day retention remains untested until the learner returns. Offer a suggested review interval if helpful; do not create reminders or persistent tracking without a request.

## Checkpoint on pause

Keep the document/edition and verified locations, remaining map, taught nodes, demonstrated versus untested understanding, pending question, and next action. Restore from the conversation or an authorized checkpoint. If continuity is unavailable, ask only for the last concept or checkpoint; never claim to remember a previous answer that is absent.
