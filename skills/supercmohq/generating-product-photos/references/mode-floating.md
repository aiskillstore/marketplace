# Floating product

**Use when:** the product hangs in mid-air with nothing holding it up. If gravity still applies and
the drama comes from somewhere else — staging, angle, light or whatever else is carrying the shot —
that is **hero** or **creative**.

## What this mode is

The product suspended in mid-air with no visible support.

**In frame:** the product airborne, a few companion elements airborne with it, their displaced
shadows, and the surface beneath.
**Avoid:** anything touching the product — a support, a mount, a hand, a wire — and any contact
shadow.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish its shape and proportions and — because a
square-on packshot has never seen it — what its underside looks like. Lifted into the air, that base
turns toward the lens.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Shot type and camera height:** at or below the product, so the gap beneath it and the surface its
shadow falls on both stay in frame.

**Subject and suspension:** which object, how far above what surface, with more air below it than
above. Nothing touches it — no support, no mount, no hand, no wire.

**Underside:** what the base looks like. This is the one product fact the prompt must state, because
the reference has never seen it.

**Shadow:** one cast shadow on the surface, offset from the point directly below the product, with a
direction and a distance. Lighter and softer than a shadow on the ground, and no contact shadow
anywhere.

**Motion:** whatever movement the shot has caught part-way through. Held perfectly still, the product
reads as a cut-out pasted on.

**Companion elements:** : Surround the product with floating props that reflect its function or aesthetic vibe (ranging from raw ingredients and liquid splashes to thematic items like luxury fabrics). Suspend these items at varying heights and distances, ensuring each casts a distinct shadow on the surface below. Limit to a maximum of 5 unique element types (multiples of the same item are fine) to keep the scene uncluttered.

**Surface and background:** named colour and material, plain enough for the displaced shadows to
read. A tone that shifts across the frame rather than one flat value gives the air some depth.

**Lighting:** where the key sits and how high, and colour temperature in Kelvin. A broad soft key
forgives a wider range of shadow positions than a hard one. Where the edge would otherwise merge
into the background, a second source behind and to one side lifts it clear.

**Camera and lens:** focal length, aperture, and what stays sharp.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself except what its underside looks like — the fixed block and
  the reference photo carry everything else.
- **Nothing touches down.** A visible support or a resting object breaks the illusion, so keep
  everything fully airborne — a half-suspended frame reads as a mistake. Nothing rests on anything,
  nothing leans.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- Concrete and named: "warm mid-grey seamless paper", not "a neutral background".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible from the angle you asked for.
