# Seasonal

**Use when:** the occasion is what the frame is selling — a holiday, a season, a gifting or
end-of-season push, whatever dated moment the brief is built around.

## What this mode is

A product frame built around a dated buying occasion, where the occasion itself is what is being
sold — a holiday, a season, a gifting or end-of-season push.

**In frame:** the product, plus staging that says which moment this is.
**Avoid:** anything that reads as shipping with the product but does not; the occasion's stock colour
scheme in place of the brand's own; every motif of the season stacked into one frame.

## Read the product first

Start from the object. From the reference, establish the product's own palette — the seasonal shift is
that palette moved toward the occasion, not the occasion's stock colours dropped on top of it.

## Prompt template

Two parts. The first is fixed and goes into every prompt unchanged. The second is what you write.

**Fixed — include whenever a reference photo is attached:**

    The attached image is the exact product. Do not modify, regenerate or redraw it. Its shape,
    colour, label, text, texture and design all stay as they are, and it keeps the state it is in —
    closed stays closed, open stays open. Build only the scene around it.

**Written — the scene only, in this order:**

**Shot type and composition:** the camera height, where the product sits, and how much of the frame
it takes. Frame loose.

**Theme:** which occasion the frame is selling, named outright rather than implied by a colour, and
the single element carrying it. If you stack every motif of the season, then you get a seasonal scene that
happens to contain a product and we do not want that.

**Surface:** something with give that the product rests into, and how far it sinks.

**Props and styling:** what and/or who stands around the product. Should be in sync with the seasonal theme.

**Colour palette:** the set, props and lighting in the brand's own colours shifted toward the
season. The product's own colours stay as they are. The occasion's stock scheme
reads as a costume.

**Lighting:** quality and colour temperature in Kelvin, belonging to the same season as everything
else in frame. Cold-weather props under summer light is the error a viewer catches fastest.

**Focal point:** what holds the product as the subject, since the frame is prop-dense by design.

**Camera and lens:** focal length, aperture, and what stays sharp.

**Atmosphere:** the feeling of the occasion.

**Rules for the written part**

- Say nothing about the product itself — no colour, no material, no parts. The fixed block and the
  reference photo carry all of it.
- **Writing the light:**
  - Where it comes from and how it behaves, never what it reveals. A lighting sentence that reaches
    for a ridge, a grain, a seam, a pore, a scratch or a sheen has stopped describing light and
    started describing the product, and the surface gets redrawn to match it.
  - Position and size, never the fixture producing it. A softbox, strip, dish or stand written into
    a prompt is an object in the frame and will be rendered standing there. A source belonging to
    the scene — a window, a lamp, a candle — is set dressing and can be named.
  - One sentence per source, two sources at most. Each extra sentence is another opening for the
    product to get described.
- Concrete and named: "warm mid-grey seamless paper", not "a neutral background".
- 150–250 words.
- Describe only what is visible from the angle you asked for.
