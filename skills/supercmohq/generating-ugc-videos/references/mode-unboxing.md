# Unboxing

**Use when:** a sealed package gets opened on camera and the product coming out of it is the point of the video.

## What carries an unboxing

An unboxing works on suspense. The seconds before the box opens are what make the moment it opens land, the same way opening a present does.

So the video is built to protect the wait. Show the product before the reveal and there is nothing left to reveal.

Viewers know what opening a package feels like, so they notice handling that is wrong — a grip that doesn't suit the object, or effort that doesn't match what it weighs. That is where an unboxing usually comes apart, and it is covered below.

## The arc

- **The package.** The creator with the box, still shut. Nothing of the product shows. The box does the work here — its size, design, how it sits in their hands, the fact that it hasn't been opened yet.

- **The reveal.** The box open and the product out of it. Keep it legible: hands and product both clearly in frame, and one item at a time where the box holds several. This takes the largest share of the duration, and it happens once in the video.

- **The reaction.** The creator responding to what they are now holding. This is the emotional centre of the format and it belongs to the person, not the object — the product can be in frame, but the beat is their face. Give them one thing that genuinely surprises them.

- **What it does.** The video stops being about the parcel and shows the product working. Demonstrate rather than describe. **This is the part that grows** when the video runs longer than one storyboard.

## The box never comes back

Shut, then empty, then out of the video. It is never re-closed, picked up again, put back, or mentioned. There is one of it.

Whatever was used to open it goes the same way: used, put down, not looked at again.

## The package itself

- **A photo was supplied** → the opening panel shows that package and nothing else.
- **No photo** → a plain cardboard box with nothing printed on it, big enough that the product isn't wedged inside. It came through the post, so nothing gift-wrapped and nothing that belongs on a shop shelf.

**Where it sits.** Resting on a surface, not held up or carried in. Put it where this product would actually be opened, and keep the surface clear of anything unrelated. 

## Fitting the arc to the panels

How many panels a storyboard has comes from its clip's length. The arc stretches across them,
and where there are more panels than stages, the stage named above as the one that grows takes
the extra.

- **One storyboard** — the package, the reveal, the reaction, what it does.
- **More than one** — the package, the reveal and the reaction all sit in the first sheet, so the wait is short and the reveal lands early. Everything after them is what the product does: put to use, tried, measured against what the box promised.

Each sheet follows from the last, so the finished video reads as one story rather than a set of clips in a row.

The box is gone by then and stays gone. Nothing is opened or unwrapped again.

## The ending

The video ends on the last spoken line. No sign-off, nothing pointing at a caption or a link.
