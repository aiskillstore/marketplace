# Logo review checklist

Apply only the sections relevant to the chosen mode. Inspect an actual rendered artifact for every visual claim. Record untested items as `not run`, never as passes.

## Applicability

- [ ] At least two distinct brand signals deserve expression.
- [ ] One plausible shared structure carries those signals.
- [ ] Fusion improves the idea without harming required-script legibility, category trust, or target-size recognition.
- [ ] A simpler wordmark, single symbol, or identity-system route was considered.

If any of the first three checks fail, reject fusion as the design strategy. In critique or compare mode, record this as the root finding.

## Family diversity

- [ ] At least three families use different semantic pairings or interpretations.
- [ ] Those families also use different dominant silhouettes or skeletons.
- [ ] At least three fusion methods appear across the set.
- [ ] A method change alone is counted as a variant, not a family.
- [ ] At least one family challenges the obvious category cliché.

## Fusion and form

- [ ] Name one structural move in one sentence.
- [ ] Make one contour, stroke, void, or module carry two meanings.
- [ ] Keep the readings structurally dependent rather than overlapping complete icons.
- [ ] Preserve one dominant silhouette and visual rhythm.
- [ ] Make weights, masses, angles, radii, terminals, and curves follow one system.
- [ ] Keep counters open and check optical centering.
- [ ] Remove decorative-only detail.

## Reduction and variants

- [ ] Test the smallest committed size and every promised breakpoint.
- [ ] Preserve the primary reading and essential fusion at the full-mark minimum size.
- [ ] Provide a responsive micro mark and switch point when the full fusion cannot survive reduction.
- [ ] Test solid black, reverse white, grayscale, and silhouette-only.
- [ ] Check relevant masks, crops, and safe areas for app icons, avatars, favicons, or signage.
- [ ] Confirm that color supports rather than rescues the geometry.

## Cultural, linguistic, and category safety

- [ ] Check for accidental category signals, regulated or institutional symbols, offensive forms, taboo associations, and misleading promises in target markets.
- [ ] Check required scripts for legibility, reading order, confusables, unintended characters, and altered meaning.
- [ ] Use authoritative form references for unfamiliar scripts.
- [ ] Obtain a fluent reader or native/cultural review before production release when the designer cannot confidently read the script or assess the target culture.
- [ ] Mark the result `provisional` until that review occurs.

Reject any known severe cultural harm, regulated-symbol misuse, category misrepresentation, or required-script misreading. Do not let aesthetic scores override this gate.

## Similarity and prior-art screen

- [ ] Review current same-category marks and structurally similar marks before claiming distinctiveness.
- [ ] Record sources, access dates, recurring clichés, and an avoid map.
- [ ] Search the selected direction by silhouette, letter or symbol pairing, negative-space construction, and category—not by color alone.
- [ ] Compare dominant silhouette, proportions, internal cuts, negative-space placement, and fusion mechanism.
- [ ] Record unresolved similarity concerns and redesign decisions.

Reject or materially rework a direction that shares both the dominant silhouette and a distinctive internal construction or fusion mechanism with an existing mark. Document generic primitive overlap as a limitation rather than treating it as proof of copying. If no current screen was run, label the result `not screened` and make no originality or distinctiveness claim.

This screen guides design direction. It is not jurisdiction- and class-specific trademark clearance.

## Scoring and hard gates

Score each candidate from 1 to 5 on:

1. brand fit;
2. fusion integrity;
3. screened distinctiveness;
4. smallest-size legibility;
5. one-color reproducibility;
6. cultural/category safety.

Use `1` for failure, `3` for acceptable but unresolved, and `5` for strong evidence. Score safety positively: higher means safer. Do not score screened distinctiveness above `3` when the prior-art screen is incomplete.

Advance a candidate only when no score is below `3`, fusion integrity and safety are at least `4`, and the mean is at least `3.8`. Reject regardless of average when any of these hard gates fails:

- semantic fusion is inapplicable or structurally false;
- a required reading fails at its committed size and no responsive variant resolves it;
- one-color or reverse use breaks the mark;
- a severe cultural, linguistic, or category-safety issue remains;
- a high-concern structural near-match remains unresolved.

## Production handoff

Run this section for production delivery; mark it `pending` for concept-only work.

- [ ] Define clear space with a repeatable unit.
- [ ] Specify minimum sizes for the full mark, micro mark, and each lockup.
- [ ] Supply required horizontal, stacked, mark-only, one-color, reverse, and color variants.
- [ ] Verify clean closed vector paths, consistent winding, no accidental clipping, and a normalized view box.
- [ ] Resolve font licensing and outline or package type only as the delivery agreement requires.
- [ ] Export appropriate editable vector masters plus named raster sizes and color spaces for the stated uses.
- [ ] Preserve an editable source and document variant-switch rules.

Do not require print color specifications, lockups, or formats that the brief will not use.

## Decision

Record `pass`, `revise`, or `reject`, followed by evidence and the smallest geometry changes needed. Preserve accepted invariants during revision and rerun every applicable section.

For prompt-only work, end with: `No visual artifact was inspected. Visual QA, similarity screening, and production readiness were not verified.`
